# GDP and Economic Growth: Belize

For this discussion I chose my own country, Belize. Since the pandemic, Belize has had one of the strongest recoveries in the region. Real GDP rebounded about 17.9% in 2021, grew 8.7% in 2022 and 4.7% in 2023 (World Bank, 2025), and grew 8.1% in 2024 (IMF, 2025). In terms of the business cycle from Chapter 6, 2020 was a deep trough, and the years since have been a strong expansion. By the Rule of 70 (Chapter 7), 8.1% growth would double real GDP in under nine years.

## 1. Factors with the most significant impact on GDP

**Tourism (exports, X).** Tourism is the main driver of Belize's GDP. The Ministry of Tourism estimates that it generates over 40% of GDP. Spending by foreign visitors on hotels, tours, and meals counts as an export in the GDP = C + I + G + (X − M) formula (OpenStax, 2022). In 2024 Belize welcomed a record of roughly 550,000 overnight visitors, about 21% more than in 2023. Cruise passenger arrivals reached about 894,000 (Belize Tourism Board, as reported in San Pedro Sun, 2025). The IMF (2025) identifies tourism, trade, and transport as the key drivers of 2024 growth.

**Construction and investment (I).** The IMF (2024) also lists construction, retail and wholesale trade, transport, and business process outsourcing (BPO) as leading sectors. New hotels, resorts, and call centres are investment spending that adds to GDP directly and creates capacity for future output.

**Government finances (G).** Public debt fell from 103.3% of GDP in 2020 to 61.1% in 2024. This was helped by the 2021 "Blue Bond" debt-for-nature swap, which saved about US$189 million in external debt payments in exchange for marine conservation commitments (IMF, 2025; IDB, n.d.).
## 2. How these factors drive growth

Chapter 7 explains that growth depends on physical capital, human capital, and technology (OpenStax, 2022). Belize shows how each works in practice.

- **Physical capital:** More hotel rooms and international flights let Belize serve more tourists, which directly raises output. The limits are also clear. The IMF (2025) warns that without more hotel and flight capacity, growth will slow to about 2%. Cruise arrivals in 2024 were still about 17% below their 2019 peak, partly because Belize City has no docking facility for large ships (San Pedro Sun, 2025). This is a real example of how a shortage of physical capital limits growth.
- **Human capital:** The BPO sector grew because Belize has an English-speaking workforce in a time zone close to North America. Call centres hire and train many young workers.
- **Vulnerability:** Belize depends heavily on one sector. The IMF projected growth of only 1.5% for 2025 because of slower tourism and weak agriculture. Hurricanes and coastal erosion also threaten tourism and farming (IMF, 2025).

## 3. How GDP growth has affected residents

The clearest benefit has been jobs. Unemployment fell from about 14% in 2020 to 2.1% in September 2024 and 1.9% in September 2025, according to the Statistical Institute of Belize (SIB, 2025). Families that lost tourism income during the pandemic were able to return to work as tour guides, hotel workers, drivers, and vendors in places like San Pedro, Caye Caulker, and Placencia. Inflation also eased, falling from 4.0% in May 2024 to 1.0% in May 2025 (IMF, 2025). That helps households afford food and fuel.

Poverty has also declined. The SIB's Multidimensional Poverty Index fell from 22.1% in September 2024 to 19.1% in September 2025 (SIB, 2025). However, the gains are uneven, which connects to Section 6.5 of the textbook: GDP growth does not tell us how income is distributed. In 2025, 27.8% of rural residents were poor, compared with 6.5% of urban residents, and Toledo District remains the poorest region (SIB, 2025). Most tourism income goes to coastal and island communities, while many rural farming families have seen little benefit.
